# NOMO x YC: go-to-market and pricing hypotheses

> **State:** hypotheses to test. No pricing, segment, or sales motion has been validated.

## Buyer and user

The user is the person completing the repeated work. The buyer owns its budget and business result. A separate expert may own procedure quality, while IT or security owns deployment and data boundaries. A sale fails if any one of these roles cannot approve the workflow.

## Positioning hypothesis

"Complete [one costly workflow] faster with approved company knowledge. Keep sensitive content inside your controlled environment, prevent unapproved model egress, and show which policy authorized each action."

Lead with the measured workflow result. Treat a customer-controlled model node with no prompt egress as the initial sensitive-data deployment hypothesis, not a market fact. Do not lead with “air-gapped” unless prospects have a written no-egress requirement. Explain exactly what stays local, what metadata can leave, and how much deployment and support cost.

## Design partner offer

Offer a fixed-scope, 2 to 4 week paid pilot after discovery. Include one workflow, up to a mutually defined source set, a measured baseline, read-only assistance, expert review, deployment decision, and final value report. Exclude open-ended custom integrations and autonomous writes. Charge enough to test budget ownership; treat free pilots as evidence only when they include buyer time, authorized data, and a dated conversion decision.

## Pricing experiments

Test three packaging anchors with qualified buyers, without asserting market price:

1. Per workflow / team subscription with a minimum annual commitment.
2. Per active operator seat, with an organization minimum.
3. Platform fee plus metered local deployment or support tier.

For each, calculate customer value from labor time saved, reduced rework, fewer escalations, or increased throughput. Subtract inference, hardware amortization, deployment, support, source maintenance, and security review. Do not price per token as the primary value metric unless it maps to the buyer's budget and use.

## Sales sequence

1. Identify one repeated job and its owner.
2. Confirm current system, process, baseline, and failure cost.
3. Confirm the buyer's data boundary in precise terms: system of record, model inference, storage, telemetry, support access, control-plane metadata, and update traffic.
4. Review 3 authorized examples and define success with the expert.
5. Produce a short architecture and egress map for that customer.
6. Present a priced pilot with scope, measures, exclusions, and stop conditions.
7. Run the pilot and deliver an outcome report, including failures.
8. Convert only if the buyer approves value, deployment, and support model.

## First outreach economics

For 30 qualified conversations, track source, segment, role, reply, meeting, recent-case evidence, sample data, pilot proposal, paid pilot, and reason lost. Review weekly. If outreach creates meetings but no authorized cases, the message is too broad or the buyer is wrong. If cases are shared but no budget owner appears, fix qualification. If budget exists but the local node blocks adoption, identify the exact cost, policy or technical barrier before considering any customer-approved alternative route.

## Competitive questions

- Does the incumbent system already answer and execute this workflow?
- Can the customer configure its current assistant with approved sources and access controls?
- Would a customer-hosted open model plus an existing workflow tool solve the need?
- Is the real gap source quality, workflow usability, permissions, or deployment?
- What is hard for a general assistant or vertical software vendor to copy?

The defensible asset is not the model. It may become a workflow-specific evaluation corpus, source maintenance loop, integrations, policy patterns, implementation knowledge, and measurable deployment reliability. This is only defensible after actual use.
