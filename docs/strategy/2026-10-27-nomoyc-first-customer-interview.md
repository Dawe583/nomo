# NOMO x YC: first SME finance interview

> **Purpose:** request one 45-minute discovery interview with the directly reachable SME owner or CFO. The founder says a request can be made this week. The contact has not yet been asked or agreed to participate. There is no demand evidence yet.

## Interview goal

Find one repeated finance or back-office task by reconstructing the contact's most recent concrete financial task step by step. Record the tools, people, elapsed time, rework, and outcome. Learn the current workaround and who owns the budget. Do not pitch NOMO, local models, or the YC idea before reconstructing the case.

## Before the call

- Ask for 45 minutes to discuss one recently completed finance or operations case. Say explicitly that this is research, not a product demo or sales pitch.
- Do not request company documents, account access, credentials, or personal data for the first call.
- Take notes without recording unless the contact has explicitly agreed.
- Separate what the person saw from what they infer about other employees.

## 45-minute guide

### Suggested invitation

> Could I borrow 45 minutes this week? I am researching how small and medium-sized companies handle real finance work. I would like to understand one recent task from start to finish: what triggered it, which tools and people were involved, how long it took, and where it got stuck. I am not looking to sell or show a product. Please do not send documents or sensitive company data.

### 0 to 5 minutes: role and context

1. What do you own in the company's finance or operations process?
2. Which work do you review personally, and which work can another person finish independently?
3. How does a week look when the business is operating normally?

### 5 to 20 minutes: reconstruct one recent case

4. Think of the most recent invoice, customer, cash, or reporting issue that took longer than it should. What happened first?
5. Who noticed it? What did they do next? Which systems, files, and people did they use?
6. Where did the work pause? How long did each wait take?
7. What did you personally review or correct?
8. What was the outcome? Was money delayed, a customer affected, or work repeated?
9. What happened the last time this occurred? How many times in the past month or quarter?

Do not suggest that AI could solve the case. Ask to see the non-sensitive steps or a fictionalized example if that is useful.

### 20 to 30 minutes: quantify the status quo

10. How many people touch this case from start to finish?
11. How many minutes or hours does a normal case take? What makes a difficult case different?
12. How often does a senior person interrupt planned work to handle it?
13. What causes rework or a missed deadline?
14. What does doing nothing cost: time, late cash, penalties, customer churn, or management attention?
15. Who measures that cost today, if anyone?

### 30 to 38 minutes: tools, data, and control

16. What tool or process do you already use for this task? What does it already automate?
17. Have you tried an AI feature on this exact task? What happened on a real case?
18. Which source is authoritative when two records disagree? Who maintains it?
19. What data may not leave your environment? Is the restriction about model training, retention, API inference, telemetry, storage region, or all network egress?
20. Which steps would require your approval? What error would be unacceptable?

### 38 to 45 minutes: commitment test

21. If we returned next week with a manual, read-only prototype for this one task, which cases could you authorize us to use?
22. Who else must approve that test?
23. What measured result would justify paying to continue?
24. Which budget would pay for it?
25. Can we schedule the next session now with the process owner and operator?

Do not ask “would you buy this?” Ask for an authorized sample, named owner, budget source, measurable threshold, and date.

## Pass, revise, or stop

**Pass to concierge prototype** only if the same task recurs, the buyer or process owner can provide authorized representative cases, an expert can grade the output, a baseline exists, and the buyer accepts a dated priced-pilot discussion.

**Revise** if the pain is real but another role, task, or existing tool owns the process.

**Stop this segment** if the pain is rare, the workaround is cheap, there is no accountable owner, the source of truth cannot be shared, an incumbent already resolves it, or the buyer will not agree to a concrete next step.
