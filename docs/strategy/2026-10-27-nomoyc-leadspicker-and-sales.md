# NOMO x YC: Czech-first discovery and sales

> Status: operating plan, not legal advice. Reviewed 2026-10-27. Use LeadsPicker only after checking source, terms, permitted use, contact provenance, and applicable law.

## Objective

Find one repeatable, costly expert-gated task in a Czech company and secure permission to test it. Do not optimize for list size, email volume, or positive reactions to a pitch. The first sales outcome is a paid, bounded pilot with an identified process owner and buyer.

## Current access and gaps

- Founder reports a direct relationship with an SME owner or CFO and ability to request a 45-minute interview this week.
- That person has not yet been contacted. A meeting, workflow, user, budget, sample data, and willingness to pay are not confirmed.
- The CFO may sponsor work without performing it. Ask for an introduction to the operator and the expert who checks the result.
- Czechia is the first geography. Expand to the EU only after the workflow and buying process repeat locally.

## LeadsPicker qualification workflow

1. Write one testable company profile only after the interview: industry, size, system of record, role, task trigger, and relevant operating constraint.
2. Inspect the LeadsPicker contract and product terms. Record the data source, collection date, permitted purpose, license limits, suppression obligations, and export rules. Ask the vendor for written clarification where terms are unclear.
3. Separate company-level research from personal contact data. A public company record is not proof that an employee's email may be used for direct marketing.
4. Use the database first to prioritize public company facts and prepare warm introductions or research. Do not upload personal data into NOMO, Papayai, or a third party before documenting the lawful basis, purpose, access, retention, and processor terms.
5. Before any outreach, obtain Czech and destination-country legal review of channel, recipient, message, source transparency, opt-out, suppression, and data retention. Keep an evidence record per contact.
6. Prefer an introduction by a mutual contact, an inbound request, an opt-in event, or another channel approved by counsel. Do not send a marketing email asking for permission to send marketing email.
7. Record objection and opt-out immediately. Maintain a suppression list for the channel and purpose required by counsel.

## Why this gate matters

Czech ÚOOÚ says a publicly available email address alone does not authorize unsolicited commercial email, including email to a legal entity. The regulator also says a request sent by email asking for marketing consent can itself be a commercial message. GDPR separately governs personal data, including source transparency where data came from another source. Rules and interpretations differ by country and channel. This plan therefore does not authorize bulk outreach from LeadsPicker.

## Discovery motion

### Stage A: first CFO conversation

Use the interview script in `2026-10-27-nomoyc-first-customer-interview.md`. Ask for one recent completed case, not an imagined future use. Capture trigger, actors, systems, evidence, exception, waiting, minutes, review, rework, consequence, frequency, and current workaround. Ask which parts the CFO performed personally.

Exit only when there is a specific job and a permissioned route to the operator. If the CFO cannot name a recent task or introduce the operator, keep researching other candidates rather than building.

### Stage B: narrow the segment

Use the scorecard as a ranking aid, then interview similar roles. A first screening threshold is at least 5 of 8 qualified interviews describing the same job, costly exception, and current workaround. This is a heuristic for a next test, not statistical proof of a market.

For every interview log:

- company profile and respondent role;
- date and relationship/channel;
- last real example and how recent it was;
- frequency, duration, wait, rework, and consequence;
- incumbent tools and why they fail on this case;
- decision maker, budget source, security approver, and timeline;
- data permission and deployment boundary;
- next action that the customer accepted.

### Stage C: concierge test and offer

Get explicit written permission for minimized or de-identified cases. Have a domain expert define the answer rubric before seeing model outputs. Run the existing process and the concierge process on the same cases. Show the buyer measured result, failure cases, support effort, and data flow. Then offer a fixed-scope paid pilot with named users, case volume, start/end dates, acceptance criteria, price, data handling, deletion, and a decision meeting.

## Buyer and user map

| Role | Question | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Economic buyer | Which budget funds the outcome? | Named owner and budget path |
| Process owner | Who is accountable for throughput and policy? | Named workflow owner |
| Operator | Who performs the steps each week? | Observed task and practical usability |
| Expert reviewer | Who decides a result is correct? | Written rubric and escalation route |
| Security / IT | Which data and network paths are allowed? | Approved data-flow boundary |
| Procurement / legal | What blocks a pilot? | Vendor, contract, and data checklist |

One person can fill several roles, but verify each role explicitly.

## Positioning and sales material

Do not lead with “private AI”, agent fleets, model training, or air-gap. Lead with the named job and its measured outcome. Support claims with an observed baseline and a clear description of what is still human-reviewed.

Prepare only after the workflow is known: one-page workflow map, before/after measurement, data-flow diagram, pilot statement of work, security fact sheet, and referenceable case permission. Never promise savings, compliance, or zero egress before measuring and verifying it.

## Funnel instrumentation

Track eligible companies researched, permissioned contacts, introductions, meetings held, qualified jobs, sample permissions, concierge completion, paid offers, pilots started, acceptance, repeated use, renewals, referrals, and reasons lost. Report counts by stage and denominator. A high reply rate without repeated paid usage is not product-market evidence.
